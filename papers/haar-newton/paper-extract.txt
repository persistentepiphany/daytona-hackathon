Abstract

In this paper, we present a new modified Newton method a use of Haar wavelet formula for solving non-linear equations.
This new method do not require the use of the second-order derivative. It is shown that the new method has third-order of
convergent. Furthermore, some numerical experiments are conducted which confirm our theoretical findings.

Keywords:
 
Newton method; Haar wavelet; Iterative Method; Third-order convergence; Non-linear equations; Root-finding

1 
Introduction

In numerical analysis, finding a solution of non-linear equation is one of the most attractive problem. In this paper, we
emphasize on an iterative method to find a simple root 
 $\alpha$ 
 of a non-linear equation 
 $f(x)=0$ 
, i.e., 
 $f(\alpha)=0$ 
 and 
 $f^{\prime}(\alpha)\neq 0$ 
.
Here, we less concern about multiple roots. Newton’s method 
[
1
]
,
[
6
]
 is the well known algorithm
to solve nonlinear equation. It is given by

 $x_{n+1}=x_{n}-\frac{f(x_{n})}{f^{\prime}(x_{n})}\,\,\,\,\,n=0,1,2,\cdots$ 

(1.1)

and it converges quadratically.

Earlier, 
[
3
]
-
[
5
]
 and 
[
8
]
-
[
13
]
 derived third-order convergence methods based on integral interpretation of Newton’s method. Where,
Newton’s method derived from different quadrature formulas for the indefinite integral arising from Newton’s theorem 
[
2
]

 $f(x)=f(x_{n})+\int_{x_{n}}^{x}f^{\prime}(t)dt.$ 

(1.2)

Weerakoon et al.
[
3
]
 have approximated the integral part of (
1.2
) by trapezoidal rules and derived a variant of Newton’s method. It is, further, shown that
this method converges cubically. Subsequently, Frontini et al. 
[
4
]
 have proposed a third order convergent method
by approximate the integral by the midpoint rule.
In 
[
10
]
, Homeier has developed a cubically convergent iteration scheme by considering Newton’s theorem for the inverse function. Further, in 
[
11
]
, 
[
12
]

modified Newton methods are derived for multivariate case. Kou et al. in 
[
13
]
 have applied a new interval of integration on Newton’s theorem and arrived
a third-order convergent iterative scheme.

Recently, Islam et al.
[
7
]
 have applied Haar wavelet function to derived quadrature rules for indefinite integration.
In this paper, we modified Newton’s theorem by using the quadrature rule proposed by Islam in 
[
7
]
. It is shown that the new method
has third order convergent. Further, the new method did not evaluate second derivative of 
 $f$ 
. The efficiency of the new method
is demonstrated by numerical examples.

This paper is organized as follows. In Section 2, we discuss a modified Newton’s method.
Section 3, we establish convergence analysis for the new method.
finally in section 4, various numerical experiments conduct to confirm our theoretical finding.

2 
A Modified Newton’s Method

To derive the new method, we consider Newton’s theorem

 $\displaystyle f(x)=f(x_{n})+\int_{x_{n}}^{x}f^{\prime}(\tau)d\tau$ 

(2.1)

We use the Haar wavelet function to approximate the integral term of (
2.1
) as

 $\int_{x_{n}}^{x}f^{\prime}(\tau)d\tau=\frac{(x-x_{n})}{2M}\sum_{k=1}^{2M}f^{\prime}\big(x_{n}+\frac{(x-x_{n})(k-0.5)}{2M}\big).$ 

(2.2)

where 
 $M=2^{J_{1}}$ 
 and 
 $J_{1}$ 
 is the maximum level of resolution of Haar wavelets, see 
[
7
]
.

Substitute (
2.2
) in (
2.1
) to obtain

 $f(x)=f(x_{n})+\frac{(x-x_{n})}{2M}\sum_{k=1}^{2M}f^{\prime}\big(x_{n}+\frac{(x-x_{n})(k-0.5)}{2M}\big).$ 

(2.3)

Now, looking for 
 $f(x)=0$ 
 we arrive at

 $x_{n+1}=x_{n}-\frac{2M(f(x_{n}))}{\sum_{k=1}^{2M}f^{\prime}\big(x_{n}+\frac{(x-x_{n})(k-0.5)}{2M}\big)}$ 

(2.4)

Further, substitute 
 $x_{n+1}=x$ 
 in (
1.1
) and replace 
 $x-x_{n}$ 
 in (
2.4
) we obtain the new method as

 $x_{n+1}=x_{n}-\frac{2M(f(x_{n}))}{\sum_{k=1}^{2M}f^{\prime}\big(x_{n}-\frac{f(x_{n})}{f^{\prime}(x_{n})}\frac{(k-0.5)}{2M}\big)}$ 

(2.5)

3 
Convergence Analysis

Theorem 3.1
.

Let the function 
 $f:{{\rm I{\!}\rm D}}\subset{{\rm I{\!}\rm R}}\rightarrow{{\rm I{\!}\rm R}}$ 
 has a simple root 
 $\alpha\in{{\rm I{\!}\rm D}}$ 
, where 
 ${{\rm I{\!}\rm D}}$ 
 is an open interval. Assume

 $f(x)$ 
 has first, second and third derivatives in the interval 
 ${{\rm I{\!}\rm D}}$ 
. If the initial guess 
 $x_{0}$ 
 is closed to 
 $\alpha$ 
, then the method defined by (
2.5
) converges cubically to

 $\alpha$ 
.

Proof.
 Let 
 $\alpha$ 
 is the simple root of 
 $f(x)$ 
 and 
 $x_{n}=\alpha+e_{n}$ 
. A use of Taylor expansion with 
 $f(\alpha)=0$ 
, we have

 $\displaystyle f(x_{n})=f^{\prime}(\alpha)\big(e_{n}+C_{2}e_{n}^{2}+C_{3}e_{n}^{3}+O(e_{n}^{4})\big),$ 

(3.6)

where 
 $C_{k}=\frac{1}{k!}\frac{f^{k}(\alpha)}{f^{\prime}(\alpha)}$ 
. Again

 $\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x_{n})=f^{\prime}(\alpha)\big(1+2C_{2}e_{n}+3C_{3}e_{n}^{2}+4C_{4}e_{n}^{3}+O(e_{n}^{4})\big).$ 

(3.7)

Further, dividing (
3.6
) by (
3.7
) yields

 $\displaystyle\frac{f(x_{n})}{f^{\prime}(x_{n})}=\big(e_{n}-C_{2}e_{n}^{2}+2(C_{2}^{2}-C_{3})e_{n}^{3}+O(e_{n}^{4})\big).$ 

(3.8)

Now,

 $\displaystyle x_{n}-M_{k}\frac{f(x_{n})}{f^{\prime}(x_{n})}=x_{n}-M_{k}\big(e_{n}-C_{2}e_{n}^{2}+2(C_{2}^{2}-C_{3})e_{n}^{3}+O(e_{n}^{4})\big),$ 

(3.9)

where 
 $M_{k}=\frac{(k-0.5)}{2M}$ 
.

Equation (
3.9
) rewrite as

 $\displaystyle x_{n}-M_{k}\frac{f(x_{n})}{f^{\prime}(x_{n})}$ 

 $\displaystyle=x_{n}-M_{k}\big(e_{n}-C_{2}e_{n}^{2}+2(C_{2}^{2}-C_{3})e_{n}^{3}+O(e_{n}^{4})\big),$ 

 $\displaystyle=x_{n}-M_{k}e_{n}+M_{k}C_{2}e_{n}^{2}-2(C_{2}^{2}-C_{3})M_{k}e_{n}^{3}+O(e_{n}^{4}),$ 

 $\displaystyle=x_{n}-(1-1+M_{k})e_{n}+M_{k}C_{2}e_{n}^{2}-2(C_{2}^{2}-C_{3})M_{k}e_{n}^{3}+O(e_{n}^{4}),$ 

 $\displaystyle=x_{n}-e_{n}+(1-M_{k})e_{n}+M_{k}C_{2}e_{n}^{2}-2(C_{2}^{2}-C_{3})M_{k}e_{n}^{3}+O(e_{n}^{4}),$ 

 $\displaystyle=\alpha+(1-M_{k})e_{n}+M_{k}C_{2}e_{n}^{2}-2(C_{2}^{2}-C_{3})M_{k}e_{n}^{3}+O(e_{n}^{4}).$ 

(3.10)

From (
3.10
) we can easily find that

 $\displaystyle f^{\prime}(x_{n}-M_{k}\frac{f(x_{n})}{f^{\prime}(x_{n})})$ 

 $\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(\alpha)\big(1+2C_{2}(1-M_{k})e_{n}+(2C_{2}^{2}M_{k}+3C_{3}(1-M_{k})^{2})e_{n}^{2}+O(e_{n}^{3})\big).$ 

(3.11)

Hence,

 $\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{N}f^{\prime}(x_{n}-M_{k}\frac{f(x_{n})}{f^{\prime}(x_{n})})$ 

 $\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{N}f^{\prime}(\alpha)\big(1+2C_{2}(1-M_{k})e_{n}+(2C_{2}^{2}M_{k}+3C_{3}(1-M_{k})^{2})e_{n}^{2}+O(e_{n}^{3})\big),$ 

 $\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(\alpha)\sum_{k=1}^{N}\big(1+2C_{2}(1-M_{k})e_{n}+(2C_{2}^{2}M_{k}+3C_{3}(1-M_{k})^{2})e_{n}^{2}+O(e_{n}^{3})\big),$ 

 $\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(\alpha)\big(\sum_{k=1}^{N}1+2C_{2}e_{n}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(1-M_{k})+2C_{2}^{2}e_{n}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}M_{k}+3C_{3}e_{n}^{2}\sum_{k=1}^{N}(1-M_{k})^{2}+O(e_{n}^{3})\big),$ 

 $\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(\alpha)\big(N+2C_{2}Ne_{n}-2C_{2}e_{n}N/2+2C_{2}^{2}e_{n}^{2}N/2+3C_{3}e_{n}^{2}N$ 

 $\displaystyle+3C_{3}e_{n}^{2}\big(\frac{N}{3}-\frac{1}{12N}\big)-6C_{3}e_{n}^{2}N/2+O(e_{n}^{3})\big)$ 

 $\displaystyle=f^{\prime}(\alpha)\big(N+2C_{2}Ne_{n}+\big(NC_{2}^{2}+NC_{3}-\frac{C_{3}}{4N}\big)e_{n}^{2}+O(e_{n}^{3})\big)$ 

(3.12)

substitute (
3.12
) and (
3.6
) in(
2.5
) we obtain

 $\displaystyle x_{n+1}$ 

 $\displaystyle=x_{n}-\frac{\big(e_{n}+C_{2}e_{n}^{2}+C_{3}e_{n}^{3}+O(e_{n}^{4})\big)}{\big(N+2C_{2}Ne_{n}+\big(NC_{2}^{2}+NC_{3}-\frac{C_{3}}{4N}\big)e_{n}^{2}+O(e_{n}^{3})\big)},$ 

 $\displaystyle=x_{n}-\big(e_{n}+\big(-C_{2}^{2}+\frac{C_{3}}{4N^{2}}\big)e_{n}^{3}+O(e_{n}^{4})\big).$ 

(3.13)

Substract 
 $\alpha$ 
 from both side of (
3.13
), then we have

 $e_{n+1}=\big(C_{2}^{2}-\frac{C_{3}}{4N^{2}}\big)e_{n}^{3}+O(e_{n}^{4})$ 

(3.14)

this completes the rest of the proof. 
 $\Box$

2.5

Conclusion

This article deals with a new modified Newton methods for solving nonlinear equations.
In Theorem (
3.1
), it is proved that the new method has third order convergence. Further, the new methods can compete with other
third-order methods. Finally, numerical experiments are conducted to confirm our theoretical findings.
The new method has great practical utility.
